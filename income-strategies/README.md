# Five Ways to Bill

A revenue plan for Pizarro's Studios, ranked by **time to first dollar** rather than
size of cheque. Every play is built on tooling this account already has connected.

The full formatted version is in [`index.html`](./index.html).

### 01 — Care plans on sites you already shipped

The fastest money is the client who already trusts you and has never been offered
maintenance. Agencies price website retainers at $500–$1,500/mo; the local-business tier
bears $150–$500/mo. Sell uptime, backups, updates, one monthly content change, and a
page-speed pass. Frame it as insurance against downtime, not as a service package.

**First moves:** list every site shipped → audit each (SSL, mobile, load time, broken
links) → send one note per client led by the worst screenshot → track in HubSpot.

**Blocker:** client history isn't in any connected account. Needs the list from you.

### 02 — No-website teardown outreach

The `no-website-lead-finder` skill already finds businesses with no site, a
social-only presence, or something broken. Apollo closes the gap to the owner: match
people at each business, spend a lead credit to reveal contact, load a 4-touch sequence.
The 160 direct dials matter more than the emails.

What converts is the attachment, not the email — a two-page teardown plus a mockup of the
homepage they don't have. Quote $2,500 for a five-page build against a $2,000–$8,000
market, and bundle three months of play 01 so the retainer starts on day one.

**Blocker:** export credits at zero — lists stay in Apollo. Doesn't affect sending.

### 03 — Short-form video retainer

Traditional UGC creators charge $150–$500/video, pricing local businesses out entirely.
Generated video collapses that to a few dollars per asset. Higgsfield covers image, video,
voice, a virality predictor, and direct TikTok publishing — a full production line in one
tool. Twelve videos at $900/mo undercuts every agency while keeping most of it.

Sell to play 01 and 02 clients first; a fresh buyer converts far more easily than a stranger.

**Blocker:** Higgsfield balance is 0. This is the only play that needs money before it
makes money.

### 04 — Lead-response automation builds

The highest-margin work isn't design. It's the missed-call text-back and the form that
never got answered. Market shape is a $1,500 build fee plus $250–$1,500/mo to run it.

The `mcp-builder` skill and the Claude Agent SDK mean building a real custom agent rather
than reselling a chatbot subscription — which makes it hard to rip out. Start with one
narrow workflow, sold as recovered jobs, never as AI.

**Blocker:** longest cycle, and needs one free reference build first. Start it in week
one because it pays in week six.

### 05 — Productizing what you already automated

The lead-finder skill, the teardown template, the outreach sequence, the automation
blueprint — each is a product for freelancers doing this badly. Specificity sells:
"client onboarding system for freelance web designers," not "Notion template." Volume
sellers clear $1,000–$10,000/mo at near-zero marginal cost.

Build nothing speculatively. Package only what plays 01–04 force you to create anyway.

**Blocker:** none but sequencing. The risk is doing it first because it's the most fun.

## First fourteen days

- **Days 1–3 — Warm money.** Audit past sites, send care-plan notes. Only work that can pay inside a week.
- **Days 3–7 — Fill the pipe.** One city, one niche, 20 leads, 20 reveals, sequence live.
- **Days 7–10 — Build the artifact.** One teardown template refillable in 15 minutes.
- **Days 10–14 — Start the slow one.** Scope the reference automation build.

## What's needed to start

1. **A city and a niche** — 20 qualified businesses with owner contacts can be ready today.
2. **The past client list** — play 01 is the fastest dollar and can't start without it.
3. **A decision on Higgsfield credits** — everything except play 03 runs without spending.
4. **Perplexity authorization** — connected but not authorized; can't be completed from a
   non-interactive session.
