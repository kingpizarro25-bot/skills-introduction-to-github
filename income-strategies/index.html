The work orders

01

Sell a care plan to every site you've already built

The fastest money in this business is never a new logo. It's the client who already trusts you, whose site is quietly rotting, and who has never once been offered maintenance. You built the thing; nobody is better placed to keep it alive.

Agencies price ongoing website retainers at $500–$1,500/month, but for the local-business tier the honest band is $150–$500/month for real coverage. Sell the low end and over-deliver: uptime and backups, plugin and security updates, a monthly content change, and one page-speed pass. Frame it as insurance against the thing they're actually scared of — the site going down during business hours and nobody picking up the phone.

This is also the play that makes every other play worth more. A studio with twelve care plans has a floor under it, and a floor is what lets you say no to bad projects.

$250/mo Anchor price. $150 basic, $500 with content + SEO upkeep.
  1. List every site you've shipped, live or half-dead, in one place.
  2. Check each one right now — SSL, mobile, load time, broken links. Screenshot the worst finding.
  3. Send a one-paragraph note per client leading with that screenshot, not with the offer.
  4. Track replies in HubSpot so renewals don't live in your inbox.

What's in the way I don't know your client history — it isn't in this repo or any connected account. Give me the list of past clients and I'll do the audits and draft all the emails in one pass.

02

Turn the no-website lead finder into a booked-call machine

You already wrote the hard part. Your no-website-lead-finder skill finds local businesses with no site, a Facebook-only presence, or something broken and unstyled. What it doesn't do yet is get you in front of the owner — and that's exactly the gap Apollo fills.

The chain is: run the skill for a city and niche to get qualified businesses, then use Apollo's people search to find the owner or manager at each one and spend a lead credit revealing the contact. Eighty credits is eighty real humans — enough for a full campaign, not a test. Sequences handle the follow-up, and the 160 direct dials matter more than the emails, because local owners answer phones.

The thing that converts isn't the email, it's the artifact attached to it. Send a two-page teardown of what their absent or broken presence is costing them, with a mockup of the homepage they don't have. Small-business builds run $2,000–$8,000; quote $2,500 for a five-page site and bundle the first three months of play 01 free, so the retainer starts on day one instead of never.

$2,500 Five-page build. Market is $2,000–$8,000; you're winning on speed, not price.
  1. Pick one city and one niche. Narrow beats broad — the pitch gets reusable.
  2. Run the lead-finder skill for 20 qualified businesses.
  3. Match owners in Apollo, reveal 20 contacts, load a 4-touch sequence.
  4. Build one teardown template so each new one takes 15 minutes, not two hours.

What's in the way Export credits are at zero, so lists stay inside Apollo rather than becoming a spreadsheet. Not a blocker for sending — only for offline sorting.

03

Sell the video the barbershop can't make itself

Every local business now knows it needs short-form video and almost none of them can produce it weekly. Traditional UGC creators charge $150–$500 a video, which prices the entire local market out. Generated video collapses that cost to a few dollars an asset — and that gap is the whole business.

Your Higgsfield connection does image, video, and voice generation, has a virality predictor for picking which cuts to push, and publishes straight to TikTok. That's a full production line inside one tool. Sell twelve videos a month at $900 and you're the cheapest option on their desk while still keeping most of it, because the marginal cost of asset thirteen is pennies.

Sell it to the clients from plays 01 and 02 first. Someone who just paid you for a site is far easier to sell a content retainer to than a stranger, and the video work keeps you in their inbox monthly — which is where renewals and referrals come from.

$900/mo 12 videos. Agencies charge $2k–$10k/mo; local tier bears $600–$1,500.
  1. Top up Higgsfield credits — the balance is empty, so nothing generates today.
  2. Produce three spec videos for a business you'd like as a client. Unpaid, on purpose.
  3. Run them through the virality predictor and lead the pitch with the strongest.
  4. Price in tiers of 8 / 12 / 20 videos so there's an obvious middle to pick.

What's in the way Higgsfield balance is 0 credits on a Plus plan. This play is fully blocked until you top up — it's the only one of the five that needs money before it makes money.

04

Build the thing that answers their leads at 9pm

The highest-margin work available to you isn't design at all. It's wiring up the boring automation a local business bleeds money without: the missed-call text-back, the form that never got answered, the quote that took three days. Agencies charge a $1,500 build fee plus $250–$1,500/month to run it, and for a single-workflow SMB engagement that's the settled market shape.

You have an unfair advantage here that most freelancers don't: the mcp-builder skill and the Claude Agent SDK mean you can build a real, custom lead-response agent rather than reselling somebody's chatbot subscription. Wire it into their existing tools and it's genuinely hard to rip out — which is the definition of a retainer worth having.

Start with one narrow workflow, not a platform. "Every missed call gets a text in 60 seconds, and every web form gets a reply before you've read it" is a sentence a plumber understands and will pay for. Sell it as recovered jobs, never as AI.

$1,500 + $500/mo Build fee plus run fee — the 2026 default shape for SMB automation.
  1. Choose one vertical from play 02 where missed calls obviously cost real money.
  2. Build the workflow once, free, for a friendly client. That's your demo and your case study.
  3. Instrument it — leads captured, response time, jobs recovered. The numbers close the next five.
  4. Sell it to the same list. Same buyers, one tier up.

What's in the way Longest sales cycle of the five, and it needs one reference build before anyone signs. Start it in week one precisely because it pays in week six.

05

Sell the system, not just the hours

You've already built something other people want: a working lead-generation skill, and soon a teardown template, an outreach sequence, and an automation blueprint. Every one of those is a product for the thousands of freelancers doing exactly what you do, badly.

The template market rewards specificity over polish — "Notion template" sells nothing, "client onboarding system for freelance web designers" sells. Volume sellers land in the $1,000–$10,000/month range, and the marginal cost of every sale after the first is effectively zero. This won't replace client work this quarter. It's the thing that keeps paying when you take a month off.

Critically: build none of this speculatively. Package only what plays 01–04 force you to create anyway. The outreach sequence you write in week two becomes a product in week eight, and you've already been paid once for making it.

$49–199 Per pack. Templates clear $10–$200; bundles carry the higher end.
  1. Keep every asset you make for plays 01–04 in a reusable form from day one.
  2. Bundle the strongest three into one specific offer for one specific buyer.
  3. Write the sales page from the case study, not from features.
  4. Ship it once plays 01–03 are actually earning. Not before.

What's in the way Nothing but sequencing — and the real risk is doing this first because it's the most fun. It is last for a reason.
